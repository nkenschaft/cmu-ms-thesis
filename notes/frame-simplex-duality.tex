\subsection{Weighted Frames and Equivariant Maps into Simplices}\label{sec:frame-simplex-duality}

In this section, we will explore the duality between (continuous) weighted frames and certain equivariant maps into simplices.
In particular, consider the setting of a $G$-space $X$ where $G$ is finite.
Then $\PP(G)$, the space of probability measures of $G$, is just the $(\card{G}-1)$-simplex, since we can label the standard basis vectors of $\R^{\card{G}}$ as $e_g$ for $g \in G$ and then write
\[
    \Delta^G := \Delta^{\card{G} - 1} = \set{\sum_{g \in G} x_g e_g \in R^{\card{G}} : \forall g \in G, x_g \in [0,1] \tand \sum_{g \in G} x_g = 1}
\]
which gives us the bijection
\[
    \PP(G) \ni \mu \mapsto \sum_{g \in G} \mu(g) e_g \in \Delta^G.
\]
Because of this, I will treat measures $\mu \in \PP(G)$ as vectors in $\R^{\card{G}}$.
Similarly, a function $f : G \to \R$ is equivalently a vector $f \in \R^{\card{G}}$.
This gives us that
\[
    \int_G f d \mu
    = \sum_{g \in G} \mu(g) f(g)
    = \mu \cdot f
\]
where $\cdot$ is the standard Euclidean dot product.

Going forward, we will assume that $G$ acts freely on $X$, which means that $\mu_x = \angles{\mu_x}_y$ for any weighted frame $\mu_\bullet$ and $x,y \in X$ since $G_y = \set{e}$.
In particular, for some convergent sequence $x_n \to x \in X$, this means $\angles{\mu_{x_n}}_x \wto \angles{\mu_x}_x$ if and only if $\mu_{x_n} \wto \mu_x$.
But this means that for all $f \in \R^{\card{G}}$, $\mu_{x_n} \cdot f \to \mu_x \cdot f$, and thus $\mu_{x_n} \to \mu_x$ under the usual Euclidean topology.
Hence, continuity of the weighted frame $\mu_\bullet$ is equivalent to continuity of the map $x \mapsto \mu_x$.
In summary, a continuous weighted frame for $G$ acting on $X$ is simply a continuous map from $X$ into $\Delta^G$ when $G$ is finite and acts freely on $X$.

We also want (weakly) equivariant weighted frames, which in this case are weighted frames such that $\mu_{hx} (g) = \mu_x (\inv{h} g)$ (equivariant and weakly equivariant frames are the same when $G$ acts freely).
Now let $\varphi : G \to \Sym(G)$ be the injective group homomorphism $\varphi(g) = \inv{g} \cdot -$ where $\Sym(G)$ is the symmetric group on $G$, so we want $\mu_{hx}(g) = \mu_x (\varphi(h) g)$.
But $\Sym(G)$ acts naturally on $\Delta^G$ via permutation, so an equivariant weighted frame is really just a $G$-equivariant map from $X$ to $\Delta^G$ where $G$ acts on $x \in \Delta^G$ by $g \cdot x := \varphi(g) x$.
In this setting, we have the following theorem.


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:equi-map-iff-sum}
    A map $f : X \to \Delta^G$ is $G$-equivariant if and only if there is some map $\alpha : X \to [0,1]$ such that $\sum_{g \in G} \alpha(gx) = 1$ for all $x \in X$ and
    \[
        f(x) = \sum_{g \in G} \alpha(\inv{g} x) e_g.
    \]
    \begin{proof}
        Note that the reverse direction is trivial, so we just prove the forward direction.
        Since $\card{G} < \infty$, we must be able to write $f(x) = \sum_{g \in G} f_g(x) e_g$ for continuous $f_g : X \to [0,1]$ with $\sum_{g \in G} f_g(x) = 1$ at all $x \in X$.
        Then the equivariance of $f$ gives us
        \[
            \sum_{g \in G} f_g(hx) e_g
            = f(hx)
            = h f(x)
            = h \sum_{g \in G} f_g(x) e_g
            = \sum_{g \in G} f_{\inv{h}g}(x) e_g.
        \]
        Hence, for each $g,h \in G$ and $x \in X$ we have $f_g(hx) = f_{\inv{h}g} (x)$, so we can take $\alpha := f_e$.
        Observe that $\alpha$ is certainly continuous, and we conclude by computing
        \[
            \sum_{g \in G} \alpha(\inv{g} x)
            = \sum_{g \in G} f_e (\inv{g} x)
            = \sum_{g \in G} f_g (x)
            = f(x).
        \]
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}


We would generally like to construct equivariant frames that are computationally efficient, so we make the following definition.

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{cardinality} of a weighted frame $\mu_\bullet$ is
    \[
        \card{\mu_\bullet} := \sup_{x \in X} \min \set{n \in \N: \exists g_1,...,g_n \in G, \alpha_1,...,\alpha_n \in \R, \mu_x = \sum_{i \leq n} \alpha_i \delta_{g_i} \tand \sum_{i \leq n} \alpha_i = 1}.
    \]
    In other words, $\card{\mu_\bullet}$ is the minimum number of Dirac measures needed to represent $\mu_x$ for all $x \in X$.
    We take $\card{\mu_\bullet} = \infty$ if $\mu_x$ cannot be written as a finite sum of Dirac measures for some $x \in X$.
\end{definition}

And we can make a similar definition for canonicalization frames.

\begin{definition}
    The \emph{cardinality} of a weighted canonicalization frame $\nu_\bullet$ is
    \[
        \card{\nu_\bullet} := \sup_{x \in X} \min \set{n \in \N: \exists x_1,...,x_n \in X, \alpha_1,...,\alpha_n \in \R, \mu_x = \sum_{i \leq n} \alpha_i \delta_{x_i} \tand \sum_{i \leq n} \alpha_i = 1}.
    \]
    We take $\card{\nu_\bullet} = \infty$ if $\nu_x$ cannot be written as a finite sum of Dirac measures for some $x \in X$.
\end{definition}


Note that the frames constructed in theorem \ref{thm:frame-equivalence} satisfy $\card{\mu_\bullet} = \card{\nu_\bullet}$ when $G$ acts freely on $X$, and this continues to hold when $G$ is infinite.


Observe then that $\card{\mu_\bullet} = k$ means that the image of $x \mapsto \mu_x$ lives in the $(k-1)$-skeleton of $\Delta^G$, and notably is not fully contained in the $(k-2)$-skeleton.
Hence, we have the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equi-frame-iff-equi-map}
    Let $X$ be a free $G$-space for a finite group $G$.
    Then there exists a continuous equivariant weighted frame $\mu_\bullet : X \to \PP(G)$ with $\card{\mu_\bullet} = k$ if and only if there exists a $G$-equivariant map $f : X \to_G (\Delta^G)^{(k-1)}$.
\end{proposition}


We now make an observation about how $G$ acts on $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ for various $k$.


\begin{proposition}\label{prop:free-on-simplex-skeleton-iff-no-small-subgroups}
    $G$ acts freely on $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ if and only if $G$ has no nontrivial subgroup $H$ such that $\card{H} \leq k+1$.
    \begin{proof}
        First, assume there is some subgroup $H \sub G$ with $2 \leq \card{H} \leq k+1$ and fix some $h \in H$.
        Observe that $\sum_{g \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{g} \in (\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ since $\card{H} \leq k+1$ and
        \[
            h \cdot \sum_{g \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{g}
            = \sum_{g \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{\inv{h}g}
            = \sum_{g \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{g}
        \]
        so $\sum_{g \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{g}$ has nontrivial stabilizer (namely, its stabilizer is $H$).
        
        Conversely, assume that there is some $x = \sum_{g \in G} x_g e_g \in (\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ with nontrivial stabilizer.
        Note that $G$ acts on $x$ via cyclic permutations of the nonzero coordinates, of which there are at most $k+1$, so we must have that $\card{G_x} \leq k+1$.
        Hence, $G_x$ is exactly the nontrivial subgroup we desire.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}


\begin{theorem}[Dold's Theorem \cite{dold1983simpleproofsborsukulam}, as in Theorem 6.2.6 of \cite{matousek2007usingborsukulam}]\label{thm:dold}
    Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite group, let $X$ be an $n$-connected $G$-space, and let $Y$ be a free $G$-space of dimension at most $n$.
    Then there are no $G$-equivariant maps from $X$ to $Y$.
\end{theorem}


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:frame-card-geq-min-prime-divisor}
    Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite group, let $p$ be the least prime divisor of $\card{G}$, and let $X$ be an $n$-connected $G$-space for $n \geq p$.
    Then there is no robust frame $\mu_\bullet : X \to \PP(G)$ with $\card{\mu_\bullet} < p$.
    \begin{proof}
        Since $p$ is the least prime divisor of $\card{G}$, there can be no subgroups $H$ of $G$ with $\card{H} < p$ and hence $G$ acts freely on $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ for any $k \leq p$ by proposition \ref{prop:free-on-simplex-skeleton-iff-no-small-subgroups}.
        Clearly $\dim (\Delta^G)^{(k)} = k \leq p \leq n$, so by theorem \ref{thm:dold} we then have that there can be no $G$-equivariant map from $X$ to $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$.
        Finally, by proposition \ref{prop:equi-frame-iff-equi-map} we conclude that there can be no robust frame $\mu_\bullet : X \to \PP(G)$ with $\card{\mu_\bullet} = k+1$ for any $1 \leq k \leq p$.
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
    If $X$ is simply connected then there are no nontrivial robust frames when $G = \Z_p$ for $p$ prime.
\end{corollary}

In \cite{ozaydin1987equivariantmaps}, \"Ozaydin provides the following useful result.


\begin{theorem}[Lemma 4.1 of \cite{ozaydin1987equivariantmaps}]\label{thm:Ozaydin}
    Let $X$ be a $(d+1)$-dimensional free $G$-CW complex and let $Y$ be a $(d-1)$-connected $G$-CW complex.
    If $d = 1$ also assume that $\pi_1(Y)$ is Abelian.
    Then there exists a $G$-equivariant map from $X$ into $Y$ if and only if there are $H$-equivariant maps from $X$ into $Y$ for every $p$-Sylow subgroup $H \sub G$.
\end{theorem}


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:subgroup-equi-skeleton-map}
    Let $X$ be a $G$-space, let $H \sub G$ be a subgroup, and let $k \in \N$ such that $k + 1 \geq \card{H}$.
    Then there exists an $H$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$.
    \begin{proof}
        Define the map $f : X \to (\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ for all $x \in X$ by
        \[
            f(x) = \sum_{h \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_h.
        \]
        Observe that indeed $\im(f) \sub (\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ since $\card{H} \leq k + 1$.
        Additionally, $f$ is $H$-equivariant since $f$ is constant and for any $g \in H$,
        \[
            g \cdot \sum_{h \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_h
            = \sum_{h \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_{\inv{g} h}
            = \sum_{h \in H} \f{1}{\card{H}} e_h
        \]
        so the single value $f$ takes is fixed by $H$.
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}


\begin{theorem}
    Let $X$ be a free $G$-CW complex and let $k \in \N$ such that $k \geq \dim(X) - 2$ and $k + 1 \geq \card{H}$ for every $p$-Sylow subgroup $H \sub G$.
    Then there exists a $G$-equivariant map from $X$ to $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$.
    \begin{proof}
        This follows directly from lemma \ref{lem:subgroup-equi-skeleton-map} and theorem \ref{thm:Ozaydin}.
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}


\begin{corollary}
    Let $X$ be a free $G$-CW complex and let $k \in \N$ such that $k + 1 \geq \card{H}$ for every $p$-Sylow subgroup $H \sub G$.
    Then there exists a robust frame $\mu_\bullet : X \to \PP(G)$ with $\card{\mu_\bullet} \leq k + 1$.
\end{corollary}


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:subgroup-equi-map-simplex}
    Let $X$ be a $G$-space and let $H \sub G$ be a subgroup such that there is some $H$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$.
    Then there is a $H$ equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^H)^{(k)}$.
    \begin{proof}
        Let $f : X \to (\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ be $H$-equivariant.
        Then define $\Hat{f} : X \to (\Delta^H)^{(k)}$ by
        \[
            \Hat{f}(x)
            := \sum_{g \in H} \f{f_g(x)}{\sum_{h \in H} f_h(x)} e_g.
        \]
        Observe that $\Hat{f}$ is well-defined and continuous from its construction.
        To see that $\Hat{f}$ is $H$-equivariant, let $g_0 \in H$ and we compute
        \begin{align*}
            \Hat{f}(g_0x)
            &= \sum_{g \in H} \f{f_g(g_0 x)}{\sum_{h \in H} f_h(g_0 x)} e_g \\
            &= \sum_{g \in H} \f{f_{\inv{g_0}g}(x)}{\sum_{h \in H} f_{\inv{g_0} h}(x)} e_g \tag{by $G$-equivariance of $f$} \\
            &= \sum_{g \in H} \f{f_{\inv{g_0}g}(x)}{\sum_{h \in H} f_{h}(x)} e_g \\
            &= g_0 \Hat{f}(x).
        \end{align*}
        Therefore we conclude that $\Hat{f}$ is an $H$-equivariant map.
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}


\begin{theorem}
    Let $X$ be a free $n$-connected $G$-CW complex of dimension $m$, and let $p$ be the maximum prime divisor of $\card{G}$.
    % TODO: this is only possible if X is contractible lol
    Then if $m - 1 \leq k \leq \min\set{p - 2,n}$, there is no $G$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$.
    \begin{proof}
        To begin, let $G_p \sub G$ be a $p$-Sylow subgroup, whose existence is guaranteed by Sylow's first theorem.
        Observe that every subgroup $H \sub G_p$ must have $p | \card{H}$, but $k+1 \leq p-1$ so proposition \ref{prop:free-on-simplex-skeleton-iff-no-small-subgroups} gives us that $G_p$ must act freely on $(\Delta^{G_p})^{(k)}$.
        Then by Dold's Theorem (theorem \ref{thm:dold}), we have that there is no $G_p$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^{G_p})^{(k)}$ since $\dim ((\Delta^{G_p})^{(k)}) = k \leq n$.
        Lemma \ref{lem:subgroup-equi-map-simplex} then implies that there is no $G_p$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$, so by \"Ozaydin's Theorem (theorem \ref{thm:Ozaydin}) we conclude that there is no $G$-equivariant map from $X$ into $(\Delta^G)^{(k)}$ since $(\Delta^{G})^{(k)}$ is $(k-1)$-connected and $k-1 \geq m-2$.
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}

We now return to an idea from chapter \ref{ch:canonicalization} that finds new use.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:secat-induces-frame}
    Let $X$ be a $G$-space such that $X/G$ is locally compact and Hausdorff.
    Furthermore, let $\set{U_i}_{i \leq n}$ for $n \in \N$ be a collection of open sets in $X/G$ such that $X/G = \cup_{i \leq n} U_i$ with local sections $s_i : U_i \to X$ of the quotient $q : X \to X/G$.
    Then there exists a robust canonicalization frame $\nu_\bullet$ such that $\card{\nu_\bullet} \leq n$.
    \begin{proof}
        Since $X/G$ is locally compact and Hausdorff, there exists a partition of unity $\set{\varphi_i}_{i \leq n}$ subordinate to $\set{U_i}_{i \leq n}$.
        We then define $\nu_\bullet : X \to \PP(X)$ by
        \[
            \nu_x = \sum_{i \leq n} \varphi_i(q(x)) \delta_{s_i(q(x))},
        \]
        noting that $\varphi_i(q(x)) = 0$ when the expression $s_i(q(x))$ doesn't make sense so we can ignore this case.
        Observe that this is well-defined since $\sum_{i \leq n} \varphi_i (q(x)) = 1$ because $\set{\varphi_i}_{i \leq n}$ is a partition of unity.
        Additionally, $\nu_\bullet$ is a canonicalization frame by construction since it only depends on $q(x)$, and for any continuous map $f : X \to \R$ we have
        \[
            \int_X f d\nu_x
            = \sum_{i \leq n} \varphi_i(q(x)) f(s_i(q(x)))
        \]
        which is continuous as a composition of continuous functions, so $\nu_\bullet$ is a robust frame.
        Lastly, by construction $\card{\nu_\bullet} \leq n$ so we are done.
    \end{proof}
\end{proposition}


\begin{remark}
    In general, we cannot expect to start with a robust canonicalization frame and produce some local sections of the quotient $q : X \to X/G$.
    For instance, the frame assigning the uniform distribution to every orbit has no clear way to extract open subsets of $X/G$ on which to define local sections, much less a way to construct the sections themselves.
\end{remark}


The author's of \cite{knudsen2024analogcategorycomplexity} give a more general definition of the underlying objects here and present essentially the same proof as proposition \ref{prop:secat-induces-frame}.


\begin{definition}
    Given a map $f : X \to Y$, we define
    \[
        \PP(f)
        := \set{\mu \in \PP(X) : \card{f[\supp (\mu)]} = 1}.
    \]
    Then there is a natural projection $p_f : \PP(f) \to Y$ given by $p_f(\mu) = y$ where $y \in Y$ is the unique element such that $\set{y} = f[\supp (\mu)]$.
\end{definition}


\begin{definition}
    For a space $X$, we define
    \[
        \PP_n(X) := \set{\mu \in \PP(X) : \card{\supp(\mu)} \leq n}
    \]
    and consequently for a map $f : X \to Y$ we define
    \[
        \PP_n(f) := \PP(f) \cap \PP_n(X).
    \]
\end{definition}


\begin{definition}
    For a map $f : X \to Y$, the \emph{analog sectional category} of $f$ is
    \[
        \asecat(f) := \min \set{n \in \Z : \exists s_f : Y \to \PP(f), \im (s_f) \sub \PP_n(f), p_f \circ s_f = \id_Y}.
    \]
    Note that this definition is offset by 1 from \cite{knudsen2024analogcategorycomplexity}'s.
\end{definition}


Essentially the same proof as proposition \ref{prop:secat-induces-frame} then gives the following result.


\begin{theorem}[Corollary 5.5 in \cite{knudsen2024analogcategorycomplexity}]\label{thm:asecat-leq-secat}
    If $X$ and $Y$ are spaces with $Y$ paracompact, then for any map $f : X \to Y$ we have $\asecat(f) \leq \ssecat(f)$.
\end{theorem}


\begin{remark}\label{rem:asecat-robust-frame-equivalence}
    A robust canonicalization frame $\nu_\bullet : X \to \PP(X)$ corresponds exactly to a section of the projection $p_q : \PP(q) \to X/G$ when $q : X \to X/G$ is the quotient map.
    \begin{proof}
        First let $\nu_\bullet$ be a finite robust canonicalization frame with $\card{\nu_\bullet} = n$.
        Recall that $\nu_\bullet$ is fixed on any orbit $Gx \sub X$, so we have an induced map $\Hat{\nu}_\bullet : X/G \to \PP(X)$.
        Additionally, $\supp (\nu_x) \sub Gx$ for all $x \in X$ and $\card{\nu_\bullet} = n$, so we actually have that $\im (\Hat{\nu}_\bullet) \sub \PP_n(q)$.
        Lastly, $p_q(\Hat{\nu_\bullet}(q(x))) = p_q(\nu_x) = q(x)$, so $\Hat{\nu}_\bullet$ is a section of $p_q$, as desired.
        % TODO: show continuity? I think we're good actually

        Conversely, let $s_q : X/G \to \PP(q)$ be a section of $p_q$ such that $\im(s_q) \sub \PP_n(q)$.
        Then we can define $\nu_\bullet : X \to \PP(X)$ by $\nu_x = s_q (q(x))$.
        Observe that $\nu_\bullet$ is an orbit canonicalization frame automatically, and $\nu_\bullet$ is also continuous since $s_q$ and $q$ are continuous.
        Lastly, $\card{\nu_\bullet} \leq n$ since $\im(s_q) \sub \PP_n(q)$.
        

    \end{proof}
\end{remark}


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:min-frame-leq-secat}
    Let $X$ be a $G$-space such that $X/G$ is locally compact and Hausdorff.
    Then letting $q : X \to X/G$ be the quotient, we have
    \[
        \min\set{n \in \Z : \exists \nu_\bullet : X \to \PP(X), \nu_\bullet \text{ is robust}, \card{\nu_\bullet} = n}
        = \asecat(q)
        \leq \ssecat(q).
    \]
    \begin{proof}
        The equality follows from remark \ref{rem:asecat-robust-frame-equivalence}, and the inequality follows from proposition \ref{prop:secat-induces-frame} (or equivalently from theorem \ref{thm:asecat-leq-secat}).
    \end{proof}
\end{theorem}


\begin{corollary}
    Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite group, let $p$ be the least prime divisor of $\card{G}$, and let $X$ be an $n$-connected $G$-space for $n \geq p$.
    Then $\ssecat(q : X \to X/G) \geq p$.
    In particular, if $G = \Z_p$ for some prime $p$ then $\ssecat(q : X \to X/G) \geq p$.
    \begin{proof}
        This follows directly from theorems \ref{thm:frame-card-geq-min-prime-divisor} and \ref{thm:min-frame-leq-secat}.
    \end{proof}
\end{corollary}


With theorem \ref{thm:min-frame-leq-secat} in hand, we have established a deep relationship between robust frames and canonicalization, tying chapters \ref{ch:canonicalization} and \ref{ch:frame-averaging} together nicely.
